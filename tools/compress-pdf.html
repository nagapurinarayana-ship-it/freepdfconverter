What this compression does

It focuses on lossless PDF structure: stream compression, recompression of supported Flate streams and compressed object streams.

What it does not do

It does not intentionally lower JPEG or image quality. Image-heavy PDFs may therefore see only a small size reduction.

Compress PDF files without uploading them

This free PDF compressor runs in your browser. Your selected document is read locally, processed by a qpdf WebAssembly engine and used to create the output on your device. There is no document upload queue or server-side conversion job for this workflow.

How PDF compression works here

  1. Select your PDF. The browser reads the file locally.
  2. Recompress supported streams. The local engine applies lossless stream and object-stream compression.
  3. Compare the result. The tool reports the original size, compressed size and percentage reduction.
  4. Download the smaller copy. A new -compressed.pdf file is created without replacing your source PDF.

Why some PDFs barely shrink

Compression results depend heavily on what is already inside the PDF. Text, vector graphics and other structural data can benefit from lossless recompression. A PDF made mostly of already-compressed JPEG photographs may have little left to remove without changing image quality.

Lossless PDF compression vs. image compression

These are different tasks. Lossless structural compression aims to reduce redundant PDF encoding without intentionally changing the visual quality of the pages. Image compression can reduce a PDF much more when it contains large photographs, but it usually involves changing image encoding or quality. This first FreePDF Tools compressor deliberately uses the safer lossless path.

Frequently asked questions

Is this PDF compressor free?

Yes. The compression tool runs in your browser without an account or paid subscription.

Are my PDF files uploaded?

No. The selected PDF is processed locally in your browser by the compression engine.

Will compression reduce image quality?

This workflow is lossless with respect to the supported PDF stream transformations. It does not intentionally recompress JPEG images to a lower visual quality.

Why did my PDF get almost no smaller?

The source may already be efficiently compressed, or it may be dominated by already-compressed images. Stronger size reduction would require image optimization, which is a different, potentially lossy operation.

Can I compress a password-protected PDF?

Unlock the PDF first when you know the password, then compress the unencrypted copy.
